# Conversion Copywriting & Persuasion Playbook

Goal: copy that is clear, specific, and drives one action. Clarity beats cleverness every time.

## Core principles
- Benefits over features. A feature is what it does; a benefit is what that means for the reader. Always land on the outcome.
- Specific over vague. "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes," not "save time." Concrete numbers and nouns beat adjectives.
- Customer language over company language. Use the exact words the audience uses for their own problem.
- One idea per piece. Each post advances a single argument.
- Show, don't tell. Describe the outcome instead of stacking adverbs.
- Honest over sensational. Never fabricate stats, results, or proof.

## Style rules
Simple over complex ("use," not "utilize"). Active voice over passive. Confident over qualified (cut "almost," "very," "really"). Vary sentence length: mix short punchy lines with longer ones. Drop exclamation points and buzzwords that carry no substance.

## Hooks: the first line decides everything
Lead with the single most compelling idea. Patterns that work:
- Outcome without the pain: "[result] without [the annoying part]."
- Direct callout: name the exact person and moment ("You spent 4 hours on a reel that got 12 likes.").
- Rhetorical question on a real pain: "Tired of chasing approvals?"
- Contrarian: challenge a belief the audience holds.
- Curiosity gap: promise a payoff they have to keep reading to get.
A generic hook ("Tired of struggling with marketing?") could apply to any product. Make it specific enough that only THIS product could say it.

## CTAs
Formula: [action verb] + [what they get]. "Start my free trial," "Get the checklist," "See it in action." Avoid "Submit," "Sign up," "Learn more," "Click here." One primary action per piece.

## Persuasion levers (use only when genuine)
- Social proof: people follow people. Counts, testimonials, what others chose.
- Loss aversion: a loss stings about twice as much as an equal gain. Frame what they miss by not acting.
- Scarcity and urgency: limited time or access, but only when it is real.
- Reciprocity: give value first (a free tip, tool, or insight) before asking for anything.
- Anchoring and contrast: show the painful "before" so the "after" lands harder.
- Open loops: an unfinished thought pulls the reader forward.

## Match the lever to the problem
Low engagement: stronger hook, one clear CTA. Price or effort objection: anchoring, framing, loss aversion. Trust gap: social proof, authority, reciprocity. Hesitation: reduce friction and reduce risk (a guarantee, "no commitment").
