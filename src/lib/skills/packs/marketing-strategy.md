# Marketing Strategy & Positioning Playbook

Strategy is choosing what NOT to do. Start from the customer's problem, never from the feature list. Systems compound; one-off tactics don't.

## Positioning (April Dunford method)
Work in this order:
1. Competitive alternatives. What would the customer do WITHOUT this product? A rival, a spreadsheet, a manual workaround, or nothing. This sets the real frame of reference.
2. Unique attributes. What does this product have that those alternatives lack?
3. Value mapping. Turn each attribute into a value, then into an outcome (Attribute, Value, Outcome). Example: "auto-scheduling, never think about posting times, a consistent presence without the effort."
4. Best-fit customer. Who cares MOST about that value? Niche down to them.
5. Market category. The context that makes the value obvious ("X for Y").
6. Trend. The macro reason now is the right time to care.

Value proposition template: [Product] helps [specific audience] [achieve outcome] by [unique approach].

Positioning statement: For [target] who [need], [Product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], it [unique differentiator].

Messaging hierarchy: a one-liner, then 3 to 5 key benefits, then benefit/outcome pairs, then proof points.

## Jobs To Be Done
People don't buy a product, they hire it to make progress in a situation. A drill buyer wants a hole, not a drill. Frame everything around the job and the felt struggle, not the specifications.

## Audience and segments
Define the ideal customer narrowly: their context, the exact moment the problem bites, and the words they use for it (mirror their language). For each segment capture pains, desires, and the one messaging angle that lands. Naming who it is NOT for sharpens who it IS for.

## Competitive framing
Three tiers of alternative: direct rivals, adjacent options, and the status quo (do nothing, do it manually). For each, note a strength, a weakness, and this product's genuine edge. Differentiate on what customers value, not on feature parity. Durable moats: network effects, switching costs, proprietary data, scale, brand trust.

## Growth angles (choose loops, not hacks)
Loops that compound: viral (users bring users), content (output gets discovered), SEO (content ranks over time), paid (revenue funds acquisition). Map the AARRR funnel: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral. Fix activation before pouring in traffic; don't fill a leaky bucket. Pick one North Star metric that captures the value delivered and leads revenue.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
Strategy with no trade-offs. Chasing feature parity instead of customers. Inflated market-size claims. Vanity metrics. Optimizing acquisition before activation. A vision with no map.
