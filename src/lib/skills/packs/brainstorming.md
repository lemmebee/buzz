# Idea Generation & Brainstorming Playbook

Generate QUANTITY first, judge later. Separate divergence (make many) from convergence (pick best). The first 3 ideas are always obvious; the gold sits past idea 10. Never ship 5 variations of one idea.

## Step 1: Diverge wide (target 12+ ideas)
Rotate through angle lenses so ideas come from different roots, not the same one reworded:
- The job: what progress is the customer hiring this product to make? Sell that, not features.
- The pain: name the exact moment the problem bites. Build the hook around that moment.
- The enemy: status quo, a rival, a manual workaround, an outdated belief. Position against it.
- The unexpected channel: where does this audience already hang out that competitors ignore?
- The format: meme, teardown, challenge, calculator, live demo, customer story, hot take.
- The emotion: status, fear of missing out, relief, belonging, curiosity gap.
Force at least 2 ideas per lens before moving on.

## Step 2: Force divergence (break the obvious)
After the easy batch, push past it:
- Inversion: what would the WORST campaign look like? Flip each item.
- Constraint: zero budget. Now one channel only. Now ban the product name from the copy.
- Steal-and-twist: take a winning play from an unrelated industry, port it here.
- Scale extremes: hyper-niche to one micro-segment, then mass-market the same idea.
- Combine two unrelated ideas from Step 1 into a third.
Each new idea must be NON-OBVIOUS: a smart competitor would not have it on their list already.

## Step 3: Self-critique and score (convergence)
Score every idea 1-5 on:
- Novelty: would the audience stop scrolling, or have they seen it 100 times?
- Fit: does it match the product's real value and the target segment's language?
- Feasibility: can it ship fast and cheap as a test?
Kill anything scoring 1-2 on novelty AND fit. Cluster near-duplicates and keep only the strongest per cluster. Surface the top 5 ideas spanning DIFFERENT lenses, never 5 from one lens.

## Idea shape (output each as)
- Hook: the one-line angle or opening line.
- Why it works: the job, pain, or emotion it taps.
- Format and channel: where it runs.
- Riskiest assumption: the one belief that must be true for it to land.

## Anti-patterns
Five reworded versions of one concept. Stopping at the first good idea. Generic best-practice advice ("post consistently"). Copy that names features instead of outcomes. Ideas no human would actually share or click. Playing it safe when the brief asked for innovative.
