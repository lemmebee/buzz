# Customer Persona & ICP Playbook

A persona is a segment, not a wish list. Build from real language (interviews, support tickets, reviews), never from who you hope buys. A sharp persona makes every asset concrete; a vague one ("busy professionals") yields generic copy.

## Specificity test
Name a person in a moment; if you can't picture them, narrow it.
Bad: "small business owners who want growth."
Good: "solo SaaS founder shipping at 11pm, 5-15 paying users, doing all marketing alone between feature work."

## ICP frame
- Context: stage/size, industry, tools they already cobble together.
- Trigger moment: when the problem bites (launch week, churn spike) or what tips them to buy (peer rec, deadline, free-trial aha).
- Their words: mirror verbatim phrasing. Write in their vocabulary, not yours.
- Fit signal: one marker that says "this is them" ("posts to LinkedIn but inconsistently").

## Persona fields
- Demographics in RANGES: age 30-38, role, company size 5-50, industry, location/remote.
- Psychographics drive the message: values, motivations, personality (analytical vs intuitive), what they read/watch/follow.

## Pains: quantify or cut
Vague pain = vague copy. Attach a number or a scene.
- Weak: "struggles with reporting." Strong: "15 hrs/week on manual reports for 4 stakeholders."
- Weak: "too many tools." Strong: "juggles 8 apps daily, no single source of truth."

## Jobs To Be Done (three layers)
- Functional: the task ("schedule a week of posts in one sitting").
- Emotional: how they want to feel ("confident I'm not falling behind").
- Social: how they want to be seen ("the founder who shows up consistently").
Hook content on the emotional/social job; the functional job is the mechanism. Capture 2-3 goals too, professional plus one personal ("be seen as strategic, leave work by 6").

## Channels
List 3-5 specific places, not "social media": "LinkedIn daily, Lenny's Newsletter, commute podcasts, Product Hunt." Sets where content goes and its native format.

## Anti-persona
Name who it is NOT for, and why. Example: "Enterprise Earl: 5,000-person org, 18-month procurement, wants dedicated onboarding. Not us: self-serve for solo founders and small teams." Stops generic messaging.

## Multiple personas
Cap at 2-4; more means none served well.
- Primary: main user and buyer. Optimize for this one.
- Secondary: influences the decision. Convince them.
- Tertiary: occasional user. Support, don't target.
Default all assets to the PRIMARY unless told otherwise.

## Anti-patterns
Assumptions over data. Demographics only, no motivation or JTBD. Vague unquantified pains. Six-plus personas. "Everyone could use this." One persona stretched across clearly different users.
