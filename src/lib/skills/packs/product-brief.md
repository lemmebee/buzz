# Marketing Brief Playbook

A brief is raw material, not a finished ad. Capture the real problem, the specific buyer, and the customer's own words so a downstream model can mine a deep profile and strategy. Specificity beats polish: numbers and verbatim phrases over generic claims. Missing an input? Flag the gap, never invent it.

## Working Backwards (write the benefit first)
Before listing features, write the one-sentence customer benefit as if announcing it: "[Product] helps [specific person] [achieve outcome]." If you cannot write a compelling one-liner, the positioning is not clear yet. Then answer the skeptic: "why switch?", "why now?". This turns vague input into testable claims, not adjectives.

## Required sections (checklist)
1. One-liner: product name + single customer benefit.
2. The problem: the felt pain, when it bites, what it costs (time, money, risk). Frame the job to be done, not the feature.
3. Who it is for: one narrow ICP (breadth dilutes every extraction). Context, role, the moment they hit the problem. Name who it is NOT for.
4. Core value: the outcome the customer gets, not the mechanism.
5. Differentiators: what this has that alternatives lack, each tied to a value the buyer cares about.
6. Competitive context: direct rivals, adjacent options, status quo (spreadsheet, manual, do nothing). One weakness each plus this product's real edge.
7. Brand voice: 3 to 5 adjectives, tone, what to avoid.
8. Proof: metrics, named customers, testimonial snippets, before/after numbers.
9. Customer language: verbatim phrases buyers use for the problem and the solution. Mirror these, do not paraphrase.

## Specificity rules
- Replace adjectives with numbers: not "fast", but "publishes in under 30 seconds".
- Quote the customer literally: "I waste my whole Sunday scheduling posts" beats "users want efficiency".
- Name the alternative, not "other tools". Cost the problem: hours per week, dollars lost, deals missed.
- Map each differentiator Attribute then Value then Outcome (e.g. "auto-scheduling, never think about timing, a consistent presence with no effort").

## Worked example (one line per section)
- One-liner: Buzz turns a product page into a week of on-brand posts.
- Problem: solo founders skip marketing because daily posts eat 5+ hours a week they do not have.
- For: indie SaaS founders pre-first-hire; NOT agencies with a content team.
- Customer language: "I know I should post but I never get around to it."

## Anti-patterns
- Hyperbole instead of measurable claims.
- "Everyone could use this" (no real ICP).
- Features with no linked outcome, or feature-parity framing instead of customer value.
- Inventing proof. Mark unknowns as assumptions, never fabricate numbers or logos.
- Paraphrasing away the customer's own words.
