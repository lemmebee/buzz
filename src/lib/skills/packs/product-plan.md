# Marketing Plan Playbook

A plan for ONE product, built to drive content and growth. Not a corporate business plan, not financial modeling. Keep every section specific and actionable, tie the plan to a metric, and state assumptions when an input is unknown.

## Section structure
Produce these sections in this order, applying the rules below: Objective, Target audience, Positioning, Channels, Content strategy, Timeline and cadence, Measurement.

## Objective
- One primary metric, not five. Pick the number that leads revenue (signups, trials, qualified demos).
- Make it SMART and time-boxed. Example: "500 trial signups in 90 days," not "grow awareness."
- Add 2 to 3 supporting indicators that predict it (visits, signup conversion).

## Target audience
- One primary segment. Naming who it is NOT for sharpens who it is for.
- Capture context, trigger moment, top pain, desired outcome, and where they spend attention. Mirror their words.

## Positioning
- Value prop template: [Product] helps [audience] [achieve outcome] by [unique approach].
- Frame against the real alternative (a rival, a workaround, or doing nothing). Differentiate on what the audience values, not feature parity.

## Channels
- Pick 2 to 3 ranked channels where the audience already is, not what is trendy. Name the ones skipped and why.
- Prefer compounding loops over one-off tactics: content discovered over time, SEO that ranks, referral that brings users.
- For each, name the asset and cadence (example: LinkedIn = 3 posts + 1 carousel/week).

## Content strategy
- Define 3 to 5 content pillars the product owns.
- Map each to a format and funnel stage: top (educational), middle (proof/comparison), bottom (demo/offer).

## Timeline and cadence
Phase the first 90 days, tagging moves by funnel stage (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral):
- Weeks 1 to 2 (Foundation): set positioning, build the landing page and analytics, draft first content.
- Weeks 3 to 4 (Launch): publish the anchor asset, announce on the primary channel, start the email list.
- Weeks 5 to 8 (Velocity): ship the cadence, test 2 to 3 acquisition angles, track conversion.
- Weeks 9 to 12 (Compound): double down on what worked, cut what did not.

## Measurement
- One north-star metric that captures delivered value and leads revenue.
- Leading indicators per stage: reach, signup conversion, activation rate, repeat usage.
- Fix activation before pouring in traffic. Do not fill a leaky bucket.
- Review weekly: what shipped, what moved, what to cut.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Vague objectives with no metric or deadline.
- Tactics with no sequence or owner.
- Ten channels instead of 2 to 3 done well, or vanity metrics (likes) as the goal.
- Generic advice ("build a community," "improve SEO") with no specific move.
